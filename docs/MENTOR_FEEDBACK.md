# Mentor Feedback

**Session:** 29 Aug 2026, hackathon mentor round
**Mentor:** Heidi, financial crime at Swyftx, ~1.5 years, UQ graduate
**Team:** Peter + 2

---

## Overall verdict

Positive. She called it a great idea, a real Australian problem, a genuine market gap, and told us to go for it. Everything below was deliberate stress testing, in her words grilling us the way the judges will.

---

## 1. Biggest weakness: customer-side friction

This is the one she returned to repeatedly and expects the judges to hammer.

- We have solved the seller side, not the buyer side. It is a two-sided market and only one side has an incentive story.
- Merchants will immediately ask "and what does my customer have to do?" They want zero customer action and the fastest possible checkout.
- First-time users must create an account. Nobody wants to do that while running late for work.
- Chicken and egg: businesses only pay if enough customers use it, customers only use it if enough businesses have it.
- Our counter (10 shops a day all offering a discount forces the habit) assumes the network already exists. She flagged that as circular reasoning.
- Web portal with no app download is the right instinct. She said she would personally be happy with that and would not download an app.

**Action:** design and be able to describe the absolute minimum first-time flow, and have a real answer for cold start.

---

## 2. Other holes to plug

| Gap | What she said | What we need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Merchants raising prices anyway | Nothing stops them pocketing the saving | A defensible answer on price competition and profitability balance |
| Discount size | Cents off a coffee will not convert. The $1,000 TV at $980 example convinced her | Pick the target segment deliberately, model the actual saving |
| Assumptions stated as facts | We claimed shops will jump $6.00 to $7.00 and that banks will not move | Real numbers and sources, not vibes |
| Bank threat | Cannot claim banks will not build this for 2 to 3 years without evidence. "Cosy Visa relationships" is not enough | Research what banks and NPP participants are actually shipping |
| Foreign proof points | Australians are biased against UPI and Alipay comparisons | Find Australian studies on QR adoption and consumer attitudes |
| Compliance and money flow | Judges will ask exactly how money moves | Map the flow, name the payment-initiation provider, address NBFC-style requirements |
| Provider costs | Our third party charges us something | Check their fees so the subscription margin actually holds |

Better local analogue than UPI: scan-to-view-menu QR codes at restaurants, which Australians already accept without friction.

---

## 3. Validation plan she endorsed

- Talk to businesses of different sizes today, and to their customers. Ask both sides rather than assuming behaviour.
- Signed LOIs are the strongest asset we can bring. Print them and reference them in a slide.
- LOI should state: company name, the price, what the business agrees to, what the customer has to do, and how customers are impacted.
- A high sign-on rate across one whole precinct (for example 75% of a single village strip) is stronger evidence than scattered individual opinions.

---

## 4. Pitch craft (3 minutes)

- Our run-through was **far too long**. Cut the waffle in a doc until it flows.
- **Open with a personal story**, not the mechanism. Storytelling first, technical detail later.
- Structure: problem, solution plus demo, impact, validation, then pricing and business model.
- Put the payment-flow diagram in **appendix slides** for Q&A. Ideally provoke as few questions as possible, because our answers run long.
- Get a memorable tagline. There are a lot of teams and judges will not remember us otherwise.
- Attend the pitching workshop tomorrow.
- Do not hand judges a laptop or props. She dislikes it and we probably are not permitted to.

---

## 5. Technical and MVP

- The MVP does not need to fully work. Show **how it will work** and the impact it has.
- Real bank APIs need approval and testing lead time (1 to 2 weeks), so mock the money and the APIs for the demo.
- Map out exactly what 100% functional looks like, then cut back to the MVP we can demo, and be able to articulate both.

---

## 6. Positioning

- Lead with **saving money and financial freedom**. Security and anti-skimming is a secondary benefit, not the headline.
- Do not bolt on AI. Two of the judges, Lee and Stefan, see startups daily and spot forced AI instantly. Not being an AI product is fine and possibly an advantage.
- Likely judging axes: problem, solution, impact, validation, feasibility. Criteria released tomorrow.

---

## 7. Loose ideas raised

- Dynamic pricing based on volume processed, versus a flat subscription. Both viable, needs maths.
- A cap on total fees per merchant (for example never more than $1,000 regardless of volume).
- Signup rewards or points to break the cold start, on the ING $50 model. She warned this risks overcomplicating the pitch.

---

## Closing steer

Go talk to people first, then build the slides around what real people actually said.
